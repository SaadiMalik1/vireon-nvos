# Scientific Manual

The VIREON Scientific Manual does not document code. It documents the fundamental physics, biology, mathematics, and robust statistics that the code attempts to model.

## 1. Source Space and Volume Conduction
Electroencephalography (EEG) and Magnetoencephalography (MEG) do not measure brain activity directly. They measure the electric potentials and magnetic fields generated by the synchronous firing of pyramidal neurons in the cortex.

### The Forward Problem
The relationship between a neural source (a current dipole $Q$) and the sensor array measurement ($V$) is governed by the quasi-static approximation of Maxwell's equations.
This is linearly expressed as:
$$ V = L \cdot Q + N $$
Where:
- $V$ is the sensor measurement vector.
- $L$ is the Leadfield Matrix, dependent on the head's geometry and tissue conductivities.
- $Q$ is the dipole moment.
- $N$ is additive noise.

*Reference: Hämäläinen, M., et al. (1993). Magnetoencephalography—theory, instrumentation, and applications to noninvasive studies of the working human brain. Reviews of Modern Physics.*

## 2. Power Spectral Density (PSD)
Estimating the power of a signal across frequencies is foundational in neurotechnology (e.g., identifying Alpha band desynchronization in Motor Imagery BCIs).

### Why Welch's Method?
A naive periodogram (computing the FFT of the entire signal) suffers from massive variance. Welch's method splits the signal into $K$ overlapping segments, windows them, computes the periodogram for each, and averages them.

Because the segments are averaged, the variance of the power estimate is reduced by a factor of roughly $K$. 
However, this relies on a critical assumption: **Ergodicity** and **Wide-Sense Stationarity**. If the signal contains a transient event (like an evoked potential), Welch's method will smear that event across the spectrum, yielding invalid physiological inferences.

*Reference: Welch, P. (1967). The use of fast Fourier transform for the estimation of power spectra: A method based on time averaging over short, modified periodograms. IEEE Transactions on Audio and Electroacoustics.*

## 3. Artifact Modeling
### Ocular Blinks
Eye blinks generate massive electrostatic artifacts due to the corneo-retinal dipole. They are typically modeled mathematically as a Poisson point process (for timing) convolved with a fixed, low-frequency bell-shaped curve (for morphology), projecting primarily to frontal electrodes (Fp1, Fp2).

If an algorithm (like a decoder) is trained without simulated ocular blinks, it may inadvertently learn to classify the *blinks* rather than the underlying neural activity—a classic machine learning shortcut.

## 4. Robust Statistical Framework
When generating evidence, numerical results (e.g., "Accuracy: 95%") are insufficient. Validation requires explicit statistical measurement across factorial perturbations:
- **Bland-Altman Analysis**: For establishing limits of agreement between different hardware arrays or decoder models.
- **Intraclass Correlation (ICC)**: For tracking reliability across digital twin instances.
- **Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) Test**: To determine if adversarial noise fundamentally alters the underlying probability distribution of the algorithm's predictions.
- **Cohen's d**: To measure the true effect size of degradation caused by hardware or artifact injection.
